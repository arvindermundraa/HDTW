# 2. Week 2 planning suggestions

**Source:** `backend/content/weeks/week2.json`, step `planning`
(fields `actions_by_focus_and_setting` and `coping_by_barrier`).

Context: In Week 2, the "Planning where you'll use it" step asks the student
four linked prompts — the moment, the action to take there, the barrier
that could get in the way, and the coping response if that barrier shows up.
For the action and coping prompts, the app offers 3 pre-written suggestions
plus "Write my own". Those pre-written lists are below.

All items are marked **DRAFT** unless otherwise noted. The nutrition team
asked where to find these lists (Week 2 content, Q4).

Prompts the student sees, verbatim:
- Action prompt: **"What will you look for or choose there?"**
- Coping prompt: **"If that happens, what will you do?"**

## 2A. Action suggestions by focus area and setting

Focus areas: fruit, vegetables & beans, grains, dairy, protein foods, fats,
sodium, added sugars. Settings: dining hall, restaurant or takeout, grocery
or convenience store, a meal someone else prepares, at home, somewhere else.

### 1. Fruit

#### 1.1 Dining hall
1. DRAFT — Grab a piece of fruit on the way out.
2. DRAFT — Pick up a fruit cup from the cold case.
3. DRAFT — Add berries or banana to breakfast cereal.

#### 1.2 Restaurant or takeout
1. DRAFT — Ask if a fruit side is available instead of fries.
2. DRAFT — Order a fruit-forward dessert (sorbet, fruit salad).
3. DRAFT — Add a piece of fruit from the grocery for the ride home.

#### 1.3 Grocery or convenience store
1. DRAFT — Buy a bag of apples or oranges to keep around.
2. DRAFT — Pick up frozen berries for breakfast.
3. DRAFT — Grab a single-serving fruit cup for a snack.

#### 1.4 A meal someone else prepares
1. DRAFT — Take some fruit if it's on the table.
2. DRAFT — Add fruit from the fridge to your plate.
3. DRAFT — Bring a fruit side to share.

#### 1.5 At home
1. DRAFT — Keep fruit visible on the counter.
2. DRAFT — Add banana or berries to breakfast.
3. DRAFT — Pack fruit for the day.

#### 1.6 Somewhere else
1. DRAFT — Carry a piece of fruit in your bag.
2. DRAFT — Pick up fruit at the convenience store.
3. DRAFT — Choose a fruit-smoothie option when offered.

## 2B. Coping responses by barrier

The student picks one of eight barriers the app surfaces, then picks a
coping response (three options per barrier plus "Write my own").

Barrier labels, verbatim:
1. Not enough time
2. Cost
3. What's available
4. Someone else choosing
5. Feeling tired or stressed
6. A craving for something else
7. I might forget
8. Something else

### 1. Not enough time
1. Decide before I get there and pick the quickest option on my list. *(not DRAFT)*
2. DRAFT — Save the choice as a go-to so I don't have to think next time.
3. DRAFT — Keep the easiest version of my plan in my pocket for busy days.

### 2. Cost
1. DRAFT — Pick the cheapest version of my plan (fresh fruit, dried beans, store brand).
2. DRAFT — Skip this trip and try it the next time I'd normally buy that item.
3. DRAFT — Share the cost with a friend or roommate.

### 3. What's available
1. Pick the closest option, since a better choice still counts. *(not DRAFT)*
2. DRAFT — Save the plan for the next time it IS available.
3. DRAFT — Combine what's available into something close to the plan.

### 4. Someone else choosing
1. DRAFT — Pick what I can from what's offered without making a thing of it.
2. DRAFT — Add something small from my plan on the side.
3. DRAFT — Save my choice for a meal I'm in charge of.

### 5. Feeling tired or stressed
1. DRAFT — Pick the gentlest version of my plan — a snack counts.
2. DRAFT — Give myself a pass today and try again tomorrow.
3. DRAFT — Do something small from my plan without the pressure of "getting it right."

### 6. A craving for something else
1. DRAFT — Have the thing I'm craving and add a small piece of my plan next to it.
2. DRAFT — Have a smaller amount of the craving + a bite of my planned option.
3. DRAFT — Give the craving 15 minutes, then decide.

### 7. I might forget
1. DRAFT — Set a phone reminder for the occasion.
2. DRAFT — Put a note somewhere I'll see it (fridge, bag, phone lock screen).
3. DRAFT — Tie my plan to something I always do (after morning coffee, before class).

### 8. Something else
1. DRAFT — Write a short backup plan in my own words.
2. DRAFT — Choose the version of my plan that fits what's actually happening.
3. DRAFT — Note what came up so I can plan for it next time.
